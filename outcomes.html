Carrying the Learning Forward

As a UMSI student, you will apply what you're learning in your courses to real information challenges, building the professional skills employers value.

The conclusion of an engaged learning opportunity is more than the end of a project. It is an important time to complete commitments, recognize contributions, assess outcomes, and prepare participants and partners for what comes next. The Office of Engaged Learning supports thoughtful closure and helps participants translate their experiences into future academic, professional, and community contexts.

Completing and Transitioning Work

As an experience comes to a close, teams should review their original goals, confirm that agreed-upon responsibilities have been fulfilled, and identify any remaining tasks. Project files, resources, data, and institutional knowledge should be organized and transferred in a useful, accessible format.

When work will continue beyond the current team’s involvement, a clear transition plan can help preserve progress. This may include documenting processes, identifying next steps, noting unresolved questions, and introducing the people who will carry the work forward. Teams should communicate honestly about what was completed, what changed, and what remains to be done.

Communicating Results

Sharing results allows partners and participants to understand what the project accomplished and what was learned. Results may be communicated through reports, presentations, demonstrations, exhibits, or other formats appropriate to the project and its audiences.

Teams should work with partners to determine:

  • The most effective ways to share the results
  • The key messages to convey to different audiences
  • What format will be most useful and accessible
  • How partners and contributors will be acknowledged
  • Whether information requires consent or confidentiality protections
  • Who may retain, share, or publish project materials

Communications should accurately represent both accomplishments and limitations while recognizing the knowledge and contributions of everyone involved.

Closing Relationships Appropriately

Strong partnerships deserve intentional closure. Participants should avoid ending communication abruptly once project requirements have been met. Instead, teams can mark the conclusion by expressing appreciation, recognizing contributions, reviewing accomplishments, and discussing whether or how the relationship may continue.

Appropriate closure also means fulfilling commitments, returning materials, transferring access, and communicating future points of contact. Even when no additional work is planned, ending respectfully helps sustain trust and leaves the door open for future collaboration.

Evaluating the Experience

Reflecting on the project experience can help teams identify what worked well and what could be improved in future endeavors. This reflection should consider both the process and the outcomes, examining how effectively goals were met, how well team members collaborated, and what challenges were encountered.

Evaluation may explore:

  • Progress toward project and learning goals
  • The quality and usefulness of project outcomes
  • Teamwork, communication, and decision-making
  • Partner and participant satisfaction
  • Benefits, burdens, and unintended consequences
  • Areas for improvement
  • Opportunities for continued work or future collaboration

After the Experience

Articulating Learning and Skills

Engaged learning develops knowledge and abilities that can be valuable in many settings. After the experience, participants should identify the specific skills they practiced and be prepared to explain them using concrete examples.

These skills may include:

  • Project planning and coordination
  • Teamwork and collaborative leadership
  • Written, verbal, and interpersonal communication
  • Relationship building
  • Problem-solving and adaptability
  • Conflict navigation
  • Ethical decision-making
  • Cross-cultural learning
  • Research, analysis, or technical skills
  • Giving and receiving feedback
  • Reflection and self-assessment

Rather than simply naming a skill, participants should be able to describe the situation, the actions they took, the results, and what they learned.

Maintaining Professional Relationships

Engaged learning experiences can lead to meaningful professional connections. Participants can sustain these relationships by sending a thoughtful thank-you message, sharing relevant updates, acknowledging a partner’s influence, and checking in periodically when appropriate.

Maintaining a relationship should be respectful and reciprocal rather than transactional. Participants should follow the partner’s preferred communication practices, avoid making assumptions about future support, and request references or recommendations with sufficient notice and context.

Moving Forward With Purpose

The Office of Engaged Learning helps participants conclude experiences responsibly and carry their learning into new contexts. Through intentional transition, evaluation, reflection, and continued relationship-building, the end of one experience can become the foundation for future growth, collaboration, and impact.
